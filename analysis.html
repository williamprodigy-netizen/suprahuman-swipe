Analysis

What they do, why it works, and what is worth taking.

One idea here is worth more than the rest of the funnel. Telling a booked lead that an unknown number is about to text them, and asking for a reply, costs a sentence and fixes a problem we actually have.

Why the SMS warning matters to us

Our booked leads get texts from numbers they have never seen. Most people ignore an unknown number, or treat it as spam — so our confirmation and reminder texts are fighting a default. Suprahuman removes the default by naming it in advance, on the page, while the lead is still paying attention.

The ask for a reply does double duty: it confirms the number is real and reachable before the call, and a lead who has already replied once is materially more likely to reply to a reminder.

He disqualifies on money in the first 49 seconds

Most funnels hide the money question until the application, or until the call. Madsen puts it in the opening minute of the VSL and frames the exclusion as respect — “there are millions of other fitness programs out there for cheap and for free. We are not one of them.” The lead who cannot pay leaves before consuming the pitch, and the lead who can has just been told they are in the room the others were kept out of.

He pairs it immediately with a full money-back guarantee: “if we don't deliver on our promise to get you back to your physical prime… we will give you all your money back.” Price gate and risk reversal in the same breath — the gate justifies the price, the guarantee removes the reason to refuse it. worth stealing

His sits before the pitch even starts. That is the difference between filtering leads and filtering attention.
